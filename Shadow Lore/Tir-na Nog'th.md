# Tir-na Nog'th

## Shadow Overview

Tir-na Nog'th is Amber seen in a mirror that isn't quite honest. On certain nights, when the moon sits at the right height and angle over Kolvir, a second city becomes visible in the sky above the true one — every street and spire of Amber rendered in silver and shadow, floating on nothing. The only way up is an invisible staircase that has to be walked on faith before the foot finds each step, climbing out over open air until the ghost city takes you in.

Nothing about Tir-na Nog'th behaves the way solid Shadow does. Its Pattern runs in reverse, the way Rebma's does, but where Rebma is simply inverted, Tir-na Nog'th is *loosened* — prophecy and memory intertwine there, windows show moments that haven't happened yet or happened differently, and a visitor can as easily glimpse a true future as a false one with no reliable way to tell which. It's beautiful and it is not safe: stay past moonset, lose the thread back down, or trust the wrong reflection, and the city has a long history of not giving people back. Corwin himself walked its Pattern once and left transformed by what he saw there — the city has never been merely scenery.

## The Bridge

Here's the thing worth building on: Tir-na Nog'th was already the place where the true Pattern gets soft and dreamlike, prophecy and memory intertwined, long before the Argent Refrain existed. That makes it the natural seam between Amber's Pattern and Corwin's Dream-axis creation — not because anyone designed it that way, but because it was always the one part of "solid" reality with one foot already in the Dreaming.

If the Argent Refrain touches Tir-na Nog'th at all, it wouldn't need to force its way in. It would simply find a door already ajar. A character standing in the ghost city on the right night might catch more than an ordinary vision of Amber's own future — they might catch a glimpse across the axis entirely: the Soul Realm's chaos vacuum, Midkemia's dream-drawn sorcery, even Morrí the Crow itself, if the Crow chooses to be seen. And if visions can cross that way, the reverse may also be true — the true Pattern, which has been actively hiding the Soul Realm's corruption from itself, might not be able to hide it from *this* part of itself. Tir-na Nog'th may be where Kolvir's willful blindness finally cracks, whether Kolvir wants it to or not.

Worth deciding at the table, not here: whether `pattern_influence` should stay "Pattern" or move to "Mixed" now that it's carrying this role. I'd lean Mixed, but that's a call for whoever's running it.

## Things That Might Be Explored

1. **An Uninvited Vision** — a character in Tir-na Nog'th sees something from the Soul Realm or Midkemia they have no way of already knowing, with no clear explanation of how it reached them.
2. **The Crow's Doorway** — Morrí, or something claiming to be Morrí, can be met in the ghost city on terms neither Amber nor the Argent Refrain fully controls.
3. **What Kolvir Doesn't Want to See** — if the true Pattern's concealment of the Soul Realm's corruption starts failing here first, who notices, and what do they do about a truth their own Pattern didn't want them to have?
4. **A Second Staircase** — is there a version of the invisible stairway that leads toward the Argent Refrain's shadows instead of back down to Amber? Nobody's had reason to look for one yet.
