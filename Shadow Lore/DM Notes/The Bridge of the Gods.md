# The Bridge of the Gods — DM Notes

DM-only companion to `Shadow Lore/The Bridge of the Gods.md`. The file in the
parent folder is served to every campaign member by the shadow lore route;
this one is not, because no shadow is named `DM Notes/...`. Keep spoilers here.

Source: this shadow is the world of Roger Zelazny's *Lord of Light*. The book
never names the planet, so the shadow is named for its sky. Book details below
are written from memory and should be checked against the text before they are
read out at the table.

## Ground rules for this shadow

- **The book is the default, not the script.** Each event below happens if the
  players do nothing. Anything they touch can go differently.
- **The players are not the protagonists.** Sam's war goes on with or without
  them. They fill gaps in it, one job at a time.
- **The gods being frauds is not the twist.** The players may work that out on
  the first day. What stays hidden is what the gods intend.
- **It has to stand alone.** No scene should need a player to have read the book.

## The truth

The gods are the First: the crew of a colony ship, the *Star of India*, from a
lost homeworld remembered as Urath. They have advanced technology and trained
mental powers. An Attribute is a talent, usually strengthened by a device; an
Aspect is the state of mind in which a god wields it fully. The passengers'
descendants are everyone else.

The First hold the world by two means.

1. **Body transfer.** Minds are moved into grown bodies. This was once sold
   freely by independent body merchants. The gods have now placed it under the
   Masters of Karma, who run a mind-probe before each transfer. Loyalty earns a
   good body; dissent earns a bad one, an animal's, or none. Everyone therefore
   polices their own thoughts.
2. **Suppressed invention.** Printing presses, lenses, plumbing, and the like
   are destroyed whenever they reappear, along with the people who made them.
   Heaven's stated reason is that mortals are not ready. The opposing position,
   that the First's knowledge should be shared, is called Accelerationism.

### The band

The Bridge of the Gods is a ring of charged golden cloud around the planet.
Campaign addition: the First's transfer technology uses the band as its
carrier. A mind in transit rides it between bodies, which is why the Halls of
Karma work anywhere on this world and nowhere else.

- Body transfer does not work in other shadows.
- A player may make exporting it a side goal, as Corwin did with gunpowder in
  Amber. It needs a substitute medium found in another shadow. The Dreaming is
  the obvious candidate and should carry a cost.
- In the default story the gods eventually disperse Sam's mind into the band
  because they cannot kill him. A mind can survive there. That is a later hook.

### The Rakasha

The planet's natives: bodiless beings of energy who once had bodies and gave
them up. They crave physical sensation, steal bodies when they can, gamble
compulsively, and honor the letter of a bargain. Sam bound most of them at
Hellwell in the colony's early days, which is why he is called the Binder.

They are this shadow's native Chaos, and they are not good. They are also
prisoners on their own world. Both things should be visible.

Rakasha move from body to body with no machine at all. That is the proof that
transfer does not belong to Heaven, and it is how Sam later learns to survive
his own execution.

## Why outsiders are useful

Everyone born here faces the mind-probe sooner or later, so nobody local can
keep a secret from Heaven for longer than one lifetime. Lily and Shorven have
no karmic record, will not be probed unless they ask for a body, and can leave.
That makes two level 1 strangers valuable and deniable to the Temple, and later
to the Accelerationists, without their needing any power.

### The mind-probe and initiates

The probe does work on a Pattern or Refrain initiate. What it returns from a
walker of the Dreaming looks like dreams: symbolic scenes, echoes, nothing that
reads as a record of deeds. The Masters of Karma take this for a failed reading
and blame the machine. They do not suspect that it read correctly.

Consequences: Lily and Shorven cannot be judged, the Temple does not know why,
and a Master who ordered repeated "failed" probes would have a puzzle that
somebody in Heaven might eventually solve.

Time runs fast here relative to the Liminal and the other shadows, so each
return lands at a later row. Gods and mortals will be in new bodies. Use one or
two faces the players can always recognize by manner rather than appearance:
Tak the archivist (later an ape), and Yama.

## Visit one — Mahartha

**When:** row 1. The Masters of Karma have recently taken the Hall of Karma in
Mahartha. Prince Siddhartha is in the city. The gods are at their strongest and
most admired.

**Arrival:** Lily Lemondrop and Shorven of Shelvar are travelling the Liminal
when a cry for help pulls them out of it. They land in Mahartha's harbor
district at dusk, under the band.

**The cry:** a Rakasha, caught and bound into the apparatus of an unlicensed
body merchant, who uses it to move minds without Temple machinery. It is in
real pain. It is also a demon that will want a body the moment it is loose.

**What the Rakasha asks:** help freeing a second bound Rakasha. It speaks of
love and of a need it cannot bear, and it is convincing. The truth is a
gambling debt. It owes one of the Rakasha who were never bound and still roam
the world, and the second Rakasha's freedom is the payment. It never lies
outright: every word about longing is true of how Rakasha feel about anything
they have staked.

**Where the second Rakasha is held:** in the Hall of Karma, inside a trap built
by Yama, the Lord of Death and Heaven's artificer. The Masters of Karma in
Mahartha have been given it to test, and the second Rakasha is the test. The
trap works. If the players open it from outside, what reaches Yama is a report
that his device held a demon for a while and then did not. To Heaven it looks
like a failed design, not a rescue.

**What the Rakasha pays:** it strengthens their flames. Each character who
helped gains one level. Each is also marked, a brand on the spirit rather than
the skin. The Rakasha calls it a gift and a way to find them again, and both
are true.

- The brand is invisible to mortals. Rakasha see it at once, and it reads to
  them as a claim, or at least an acquaintance.
- Anything built to detect demons may react to a branded character. That
  includes Yama's work.
- The Master's mind-probe does not show it; the brand is lost in the dreams.
- Held in reserve: what the brand does if a branded character dies. A flame the
  Rakasha has fed is one it may be able to keep from going out, at a price.

**The official:** an ordinary Master of Karma, mortal, not one of the First.
The Temple's new hold on Mahartha is his chance. He is using two unreadable
strangers to solve a problem his superiors have noticed, so that the First
notice him. His kindness is real and so is his ambition. He does not know what
the closure will be used for any more than the players do.

**The job:** one job, with a small errand on either side of it. The Master,
courteous and visibly kind, asks the strangers for help. People in
the harbor district are coming back wrong after cut-rate rebirths: wrong
memories, wrong voices, some not themselves at all. The Temple cannot find the
body thief because its own people are known there. Would they look?

Everything the official says is true. The back-alley transfers are going wrong,
because the merchant is working with a tormented demon and failing equipment.

**What they find:** the last independent body merchant in Mahartha, who serves
people the Masters of Karma have refused: debtors, a man who mocked a priest, a
woman whose son was reborn as a dog. The cry they followed comes from the
machine.

**The choices:**
- *Report the merchant to the Temple.* The job as asked.
- *Free the Rakasha.* It keeps its word about whatever was agreed and nothing
  more. It now knows two travelers who can leave the world.
- *Free the second Rakasha as well.* The debt is paid. The players have loosed
  two demons and done a favor for a third they have never met. They leave a
  level stronger and branded, and Yama's trap is recorded as a failure. This
  means breaking into the Hall of Karma while working for the Master who runs
  it; he must not learn who did it, or the Temple's thanks at the end go away.
- *Warn the merchant, or do nothing.* Somebody else reports it within days.

**Prince Siddhartha:** at most a glimpse. An old lord in the same inn, asking
the same questions about the Hall of Karma for his own reasons. He should not
recruit them. He should notice them.

**How it ends:** the Temple thanks them and pays well. The official remarks
that the gods remember their friends. They feel they did a good thing. The
Liminal lets them go.

## The return

Years have passed; how many is to be settled when the second visit is planned.
The Master of Karma is in a younger body and a higher office, which is what he
wanted from them.

- If they reported the merchant: there are no independent body merchants left
  anywhere. Their report was the example used to close them all. The merchant's
  clients were judged; the players can meet one in a lesser body, or learn that
  one was not reborn.
- If they freed the Rakasha: it is wearing someone, and it remembers them.
- If they freed the second one too: the creditor knows their names, and
  Rakasha treat a settled debt as the start of an acquaintance. Yama set the
  trap aside as flawed, so Heaven has gone years without the demon-catcher it
  would otherwise have had, and more Rakasha are loose than the default story
  expects. Yama does not like unexplained failures. If he ever examines a
  branded character, he will have his explanation.
- If they did nothing: the same end, and nobody thanks them.

In every case the Hall of Karma was raided shortly after they left and bodies
were taken. The Temple wants to know by whom. This is the next job offer, and
this time the Accelerationists make one too.
