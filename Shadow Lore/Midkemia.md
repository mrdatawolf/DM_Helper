# Midkemia

## Shadow Overview

Midkemia is a broad, temperate world of old kingdoms and older forests — walled coastal cities and inland duchies bound together under a single crown, bordering vast elven woodlands where the Greater Path is still taught the way it was first learned: by listening, not by rule. To the south lie deserts and rival empires; to the north, mountains riddled with dwarven holds that have never once been taken by siege. It is, on its surface, an unremarkable shadow — orderly enough to farm, wild enough to get lost in, populated by people who go to temple, argue about taxes, and tell their children not to go into the deep wood after dark.

Magic here runs on two disciplines that rarely trust each other. The Lesser Path is academic — formulas, components, magic learned the way a trade is learned, taught in towers and guildhalls. The Greater Path is older and stranger: its practitioners don't study spells so much as surrender to them, drawing power through meditation, trance, and — most of all — dreaming. A Greater Path sorcerer half-remembers her magic the way she half-remembers a dream on waking, which makes her formidable and unreliable in exactly the ways that unsettle the Lesser Path's rule-followers.

**Shadow stats:** Order 10 / Chaos 20 / Dream 70. Pattern influence: Argent Refrain.

## The Chaos Intrusion

Midkemia is a mirror to the Soul Realm — the same wound, turned inside out.

Where the Soul Realm's wizards draw Order out of beings and leave a vacuum that pulls Chaos in, Midkemia's Greater Path sorcerers draw on Chaos — the raw, untamed undercurrent of the dreaming mind — and leave a vacuum that pulls Order in behind it. Every dream a sorcerer reaches into to work Greater Path magic pulls a thread of Order back out with it, and that Order doesn't belong here. It seeps into Corwin's Pattern from the Chaos side of the ledger, an intrusion the Argent Refrain was never built to expect.

And just as the Original Pattern hid the Soul Realm's corruption from Kolvir, here it is the Logrus that hides Midkemia's corruption from Corwin's Pattern — not out of rivalry this time, but concealment with a purpose. The Logrus doesn't want Corwin's Pattern to notice that a piece of its own territory is quietly turning against it, gaining a foothold of Order in Chaos's own domain. As long as the intrusion stays hidden, the Logrus can decide, in its own time, whether to burn it out or claim it.

The two shadows likely sit near enough to each other — in whatever geometry Shadow uses — that their imbalances lean on one another. A DM has room here: pressure released in one realm could bleed toward the other, a ritual in the Soul Realm might ripple into a Midkemian sorcerer's dreams, and a character who has walked both may be the only one who notices the wounds are the same wound.

## Unique Magical Properties

Greater Path sorcery is dream-drawn: it is strongest in sleep, trance, and the liminal space just before waking, and weakest when a caster is forced to work wide awake under pressure. A sorcerer's nightmares are not incidental — they are where the Chaos she draws on shows its teeth, and a bad night's sleep can mean a bad day's magic. Given Midkemia's chaos-lean and Corwin's Pattern's stake in the intrusion, sorcerers here may find their dreams increasingly *not entirely their own* — visited, shaped, or eavesdropped on by forces with an interest in what leaks through.

## Things That Might Be Explored

1. **The Unnoticed Wound** — the players might be the first to realize Midkemia's dream-sickness and the Soul Realm's chaos-vacuum are the same phenomenon seen from opposite sides.
2. **What the Logrus Is Waiting For** — why conceal the intrusion instead of moving on it immediately? What is the Logrus deciding, and what tips that decision?
3. **A Sorcerer's Broken Imprint** — a character (like Lily) carrying a broken Argent Refrain imprint may be an open door for whatever crosses between the two realms, whether she intends it or not.
4. **Twin Realms, Twin Stakes** — quests that require working both shadows at once, where fixing one risks destabilizing the other.
