# The Bridge of the Gods

## Shadow Overview

The people of this world have no name for it beyond "the world". Travelers name it for the first thing they see: a broad golden band that arcs across the sky from horizon to horizon, bright enough to read by on a clear night. The locals call that band the Bridge of the Gods, and say it is the road by which the gods came down and the road by which a soul travels between one life and the next.

Below the Bridge lies a warm, crowded, river-fed land of walled cities, terraced fields, monasteries, and jungle. Princes rule small kingdoms and make small wars. Merchants run caravans and river barges. Temples stand on every square, bright with paint and incense, and festivals fill more of the calendar than work does. It is a beautiful world and it knows it.

It is also a world where the gods are not a matter of faith. They are seen. They descend in chariots that cross the sky faster than any bird. They walk into a temple and are recognized. Brahma, Vishnu, Shiva, Yama, Kali, Agni, Ratri, and a great many lesser powers keep a city of their own far to the north, called Heaven, and from it they answer prayers, end droughts, punish the wicked, and occasionally burn a town.

## What Everyone Knows

- **Death is not the end.** A person who has lived well and can pay the temple's fee goes to a Hall of Karma when their body grows old and is given a new one. This is not a metaphor. People remember their earlier lives and can point to the house they lived in two bodies ago.
- **The Masters of Karma judge.** Before rebirth, the Masters look into a soul and weigh what they find. The good are reborn young, healthy, and well-placed. The wicked are reborn sickly, poor, or as animals. The very wicked are not reborn.
- **The gods have Aspects and Attributes.** A god who takes on their Aspect is visibly more than human, and each wields an Attribute no mortal can match: the gaze of Yama kills, the fire of Agni reaches the horizon, the dance of Shiva unmakes what it touches.
- **Demons are real.** The Rakasha are beings of fire and air who lived here before people did. The gods bound most of them long ago. The ones still free steal bodies, gamble for them, and keep their bargains to the letter.
- **Prayer works.** Temples keep prayer machines that carry a petition to Heaven for a coin. Enough people have had prayers answered that nobody thinks the coin wasted.

## What a Visitor Notices

- The cities are old, and nothing in them is new. Tools, ships, and buildings are made exactly as they were made generations ago, and craftsmen are proud of it.
- People speak of "the old days" as though they personally remember them, and many do.
- There is very little crime in the open. A person who expects to be judged before their next life behaves carefully in this one.
- Nobody discusses the temple's judgments. Asking how a neighbor came back in a lesser body is as rude as asking how much money they have.
- The gods are loved. The affection is real, and so is the fear under it.

## Places

- **Mahartha** — a great port city, the Gateway of the South, with one of the largest Halls of Karma in the world. Sailors, pilgrims, and princes all come here for new bodies.
- **Kapil** — a small, prosperous principality inland, ruled by Prince Siddhartha, an old and well-regarded lord known for winning wars he did not start.
- **Alundil** — a quiet city of monasteries and pilgrim groves.
- **Keenset** — the City of the River, known for its craftsmen and its clever, restless people.
- **Heaven** — the Celestial City of the gods in the far north. Mortals go there only when summoned.
- **Hellwell** — a pit in the mountains where the gods bound the Rakasha. Nobody goes there.

## Magical Properties

Travelers from other shadows find that their own abilities work here but feel muffled, as though the world were listening. Arriving is easy and often accidental. Leaving takes effort, and time does not keep pace with other shadows: a traveler who is away for a season may return to find that years have passed, and that people they knew are wearing different faces.

## Things That Might Be Explored

1. Who the gods were before they were gods.
2. What the Masters of Karma actually see when they look into a soul.
3. Why nothing new is ever made here.
4. What the Rakasha want, and whether a bargain with one can be trusted.
5. What the Bridge of the Gods is made of.
